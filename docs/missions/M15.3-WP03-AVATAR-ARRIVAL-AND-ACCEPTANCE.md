## 1. First Studio walk under load

### Trace

The acceptance harness now records an in-page lifecycle trace of every world command (`wp03/trace-*-world*.json`). It wraps the app's own `AvatarCommandBus` singleton and listens to her `lelu:spatial-state` telemetry. It covers:
- command issued, delivered and resolved;
- Universe open/close and the render budget;
- world-data requests;
- her position, goal, route and window obstacles each second;
- page frame rate.

`scripts/avatar/summarize-walk-trace.mjs` turns a trace into the timeline below.

**Before (WP02 code, dedicated `actions` run, `trace-actions-iso1-world.json`).** The failure reproduced even in the dedicated run, not only under the full sequence:

| t (s after entering the world) | Event |
|---|---|
| 13.4 | World data loaded (`/api/forge/world` 200) |
| 19.3 | Chat Universe opens; render budget `paused` |
| 24.9 | "walk to the studio" issued; goal (-3.99, 0.26); she stands at (0.27, 3.99); Universe closes |
| 24.9 → 41 | She skirts a `window:chat` obstacle at (0.31, 2.78), r 2.2 m |
| 41.9 | 17 s report: "still on my way (3.76 m … 5.57 m to go)" |

| 84 | Superseded by "face me", still 4.9 m from the goal |

### Findings, in the order the evidence established them

1. **The chat window is a legitimate obstacle.**
   - When her command closes the Universe, the conversation stays on screen as a real floating chat window (722×754 px).
   - It is visible and hit-testable (`scripts/avatar/probe-world-obstacles.mjs`; screenshot in the probe run).
   - The world design explicitly makes opened windows occupied floor (`LeluV2Presence.tsx` header).
   - It is **kept** as an obstacle. No collision was removed.
2. **Root cause: no path.**
   - `stepTowards` is greedy. A blocked step is pushed to the nearest free ground, which can be backwards.
   - The window footprint and the Core (r 1.55 m) form a concave pocket. She was trapped there although a free corridor exists between the Core and the Vault.
   - Reproduced in pure code on the traced geometry (`tests/avatar-world-walk.test.ts`, first test).
3. **Stale completion signal.**
   - The 17 s progress report set `confirmed = true`. That switched off stall detection, and telemetry's `arrived` read `true` while she was stuck 5 m away.
4. **Paused time credited to the walk.**
   - The first frame after the Universe closed integrated ~5 s of time that had passed *before* the command (0.27 → 2.62 m in one frame).
5. **Readiness (code review, then confirmed by the held-data test):**
   - A walk asked before the world loaded was aimed against default bounds, without furniture and from a position the load would replace.
   - A command issued before her world presence mounted reached no listener.

Ruled out by the trace:
- dispatcher timeout (the bus deadline was not reached first);
- a replaced target (the goal never changed);
- saved-placement teleport (the WP02 fix holds);
- inaccurate distance (arrival is straight-line distance to the requested point).

### Fix (existing authorities only)

- **`LeluSpatial` (the documented single source of truth for "how she gets there")** gains pure functions:
  - `planPath`: A* on a 0.2 m grid over the same walkable disc and the same `isOccupied` rule, pulled tight to straight legs; `null` when no way exists.
  - `walkPath`: follows the route in the same collision-checked `stepTowards` increments.
  - `segmentClear` and `remainingOnPath`.
  - No second navigation manager or store.
- **`LeluV2Presence`:**
  - A walk is aimed only once her world's first load has settled (`navReady`: success, or failure with defaults). Before that, the goal is `waiting-for-world` and she does not move.
  - The saved place is applied first, so it can never overwrite an aimed walk.
  - A command issued before she mounted is taken from the bus's active command, once (`handledCommand`).
  - The bus is answered **exactly once** (`resolve`). The possible answers are arrived (≤ 10 cm), stopped short (≤ 80 cm), blocked, cancelled (drag or superseded), or the 17 s measured progress report.
  - Stall detection keeps running after a report. The telemetry `outcome` then records whether she later arrived or got blocked; `arrived` is true only when measured.
  - Replans on a changed obstacle set, or once per stall (up to 3), before declaring blocked.
  - Only time since the walk was aimed is integrated (`walkSeconds`).
- The 20 s deadline is unchanged.

### Walk asked while her world is still loading (new condition)

The harness holds `/api/forge/world*` responses (Playwright routing; the app is not modified) until the walk has been typed into the real chat, then releases them 1.5 s later.

In every run:
- she waited without moving (`waiting-for-world`);
- she then walked round the Core and **arrived (5–8 cm)**.

Whether the *bus answer* came before the 20 s deadline varies:

| Run | World data after command | Bus answer |
|---|---|---|
| full1 | 9.6 s | arrived, 17.0 s |
| final2 | 8.6 s | arrived, 16.6 s |

| final4 | 7.7 s | arrived, 15.3 s |

**Unresolved cause.** In this sequence her world never painted before the chat covered it. When the command closes the chat, the world's **first paint blocks the main thread for 7.4–9.7 s** on SwiftShader:
- no page frame is recorded in that window;
- the harness's own `evaluate` calls wait for it;
- runs where the world painted first (`iso1`) show no such gap.

That first-paint cost belongs to the world scene, not to her walk or the deadline. It is not proven to be hers and was not changed (the Cosmos/world rendering work is out of scope). The 20 s deadline was not changed to hide it. A decision is requested: either a real-GPU measurement of the first paint, or a separate world-entry work package.

## 2. Phone Avatar panel close/reopen

**WP02's diagnosis ("the tap did not land") was wrong.** `scripts/avatar/probe-panel-close.mjs` (`wp03/panel/*.json`) shows, in every cycle, phone and desktop, click and real touch:
- the harness's close locator resolves to **"Close Avatar"**;
- the hit test at its centre is that button;
- `pointerdown/pointerup/click` were delivered;
- the panel's DOM closed in 0.15–2.0 s.

What lagged was **her presence**. `data-presence` left "preview" up to **4.2 s** later, so a single sample at 2 s read "preview" in 3 of 6 cycles. That reproduces the WP02 note "closed: preview".

**Product cause.** Her placement learned that the preview spot was gone only through a 400 ms timer (`useHeroRect`). Post-close long tasks starved that timer.

**Fix (`LeluCompanion.tsx`):**
- the preview ends in the same React commit in which the Avatar module leaves `state.modules`;
- the spot is also followed through a 40 ms `MutationObserver` burst, as `usePresent` already does.

Result: she leaves the spot in the same observation as the panel closing, 6/6 cycles (before: lag up to 3.2 s).

**Harness correction (separate from the product fix):**
- The phone now uses a real `tap()` with actionability checks; the old `click({force:true})` bypassed them.
- The step additionally asserts that the panel DOM really closed and that the same v005 model returns on reopen.
- It is polled within the same 2 s deadline, measured from the delivered tap.
- One WP03 harness defect was found and fixed during the runs: the window first started before the click, see final2 in §3.
- The assertion was not weakened.

Phone and desktop panel close/reopen in every complete WP03 full run: 4/4 pass in each of full1, final3, final4 and final5. The dedicated panel runs were 20/20 twice.

## 7. Limitations

1. The world's first paint after entering freezes the main thread for 7–10 s on SwiftShader. A walk asked at that moment can arrive just after the 17 s report (2 of 5 runs). Real-GPU behaviour is unmeasured.
2. Physical iPhone and real-GPU acceptance are not done.
3. The planner is a grid A* over circular footprints. It plans round what is registered; obstacles that appear later trigger a replan on the next frame.
4. The "face me" heading in replies is not normalised (e.g. "383°"). This is cosmetic and unchanged.
5. WP02 stated that "in isolation the same walk arrives in about 5 s". The WP03 trace of the WP02 code shows it also failed in the dedicated run, so that statement did not hold.
