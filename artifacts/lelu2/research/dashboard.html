R-20261118-wp02-the-best-sampler-depends-on-the-surface verified 57%

Which sampling law produces the most uniform distribution for her actual surfaces?

There is no single winner. A surface thin enough to be one strip (the disc) favours Vogel; a closed sphere favours Fibonacci; an irregular anatomical cloud favours the area-even hybrid. The constant is not the answer — the surface is.

3 evidence item(s) · discoveries

Observed. Five samplers at n=800 on four surfaces: hair roots: A 0.3925 → E-hybrid 0.0968 (coverage 100.0%); surface detail placement: A 0.3943 → E-hybrid 0.0885 (coverage 100.0%); jewelry (diadem): A 0.4092 → C-vogel 0.0382 (coverage 100.0%); procedural surface sampling: A 0.0519 → B-fibonacci 0.0277 (coverage 100.0%). The one-arm logarithmic spiral was rejected on every surface for coverage (7–23%).

Method. Identical point cloud, n=800, same seed and same coverage radius per surface; measure spacing CV, min/max spacing and coverage; reject any method whose coverage falls below the surface it is supposed to cover.

Results. hair roots: A 0.3925 → E-hybrid 0.0968 (coverage 100.0%); surface detail placement: A 0.3943 → E-hybrid 0.0885 (coverage 100.0%); jewelry (diadem): A 0.4092 → C-vogel 0.0382 (coverage 100.0%); procedural surface sampling: A 0.0519 → B-fibonacci 0.0277 (coverage 100.0%). Curls: the log law is the regular one (R² 1.000, growth 0.618/turn), while the hybrid has diameter CV 0.153 but R² 0.934.

R-20261118-lit-sampling-laws open 21%

What do the sampling laws LÉLU uses actually promise?

The matrix's result is not surprising once the guarantees are separated: the disc has a law, the skull does not.

2 evidence item(s) · research-papers

Observed. The matrix needed a reason to expect a spiral disc law and a blue-noise law to behave differently.

Method. Read the two primary sources and state the guarantee each construction provides; no implementation was changed.

Results. Vogel guarantees equal area per point on a disc and is not a minimum-distance construction. Bridson guarantees a minimum separation with blue-noise spectrum. Neither makes a claim about an irregular anatomical surface, which is why the hybrid is empirical.

How should a tube's cross-section be oriented along a curl so it does not twist?

Parallel transport solves orientation, not penetration: frames that do not twist still produce roots that sit above the scalp. The framing literature is not a placement solution.

3 evidence item(s) · technical-studies

Method. Sweep each strand with parallel transport, elliptical cross-sections and a taper; verify by rendering and by diameter statistics.
